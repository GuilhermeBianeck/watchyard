# Watchyard interface

A camera dashboard should answer three questions at a glance: which camera am I
looking at, is this live, and what happened recently? The live image carries most
of the information. Give it the largest surface, preserve the full frame, and
keep camera names in a consistent place. Use text as well as color to distinguish
live video, previews, buffering, and offline cameras.

On desktop, the camera wall opens beside recent events. On phones, feeds stack
at full width and the bottom bar keeps Cameras, Activity, and Settings within
reach. Opening a camera gives the video more space and lets you switch feeds
without closing the viewer. Its Events button opens Activity filtered to that
camera. Each event offers a snapshot and a separate play button, so reviewing
a recording takes one action. Saved recordings have their own list.

The palette uses slate surfaces, readable blue actions, and green for live or
healthy states. System type, small corner radii, and plain labels keep the chrome
quiet. On dark displays, the surfaces stay dim around bright footage. Keep
settings, device management, and diagnostic detail in Settings, where their
labels can explain what each control does.
